📚 Academic Writing — FREE

How to use: Tap each lesson to open it. Read Definition → Simple Meaning → Example → Practice. Do 1 lesson per day, or 2 if you are serious.

Lesson 1 — What Is Academic Writing?

Definition: Structured writing used to share ideas, evidence and research clearly.
Simple meaning: You are not just writing feelings. You are explaining with proof.
Example: Instead of "Reading is good", write "Reading improves vocabulary because research shows readers learn new words in context."
Practice: Rewrite 3 opinions into evidence-based sentences.

Lesson 2 — Why Academic Writing Matters

Definition: It builds trust, clarity and professional value.
Simple meaning: People pay for clear, trustworthy writing.
Example: A well-written assignment gets marks, a well-written proposal gets clients.
Practice: List 3 places where good writing can earn you money.

Lesson 3 — Finding a Clear Question

Definition: A research question is the problem you want to answer.
Simple meaning: Don't write about "education". Write about "How does morning study affect Form 2 students' focus?"
Practice: Turn 3 broad topics into 3 clear questions.

Lesson 4 — Finding Reliable Sources

Definition: Reliable sources are trustworthy, checked and evidence-based.
Simple meaning: Books, journals, official sites > random blogs, TikTok rumours.
Practice: Find 2 reliable + 2 unreliable sources on one topic and explain why.

Lesson 5 — Taking Notes in Your Own Words

Definition: Paraphrasing is restating an idea without copying.
Simple meaning: Read, close the book, explain it like to a friend.
Practice: Read 1 paragraph, write it in your own words in 3 sentences.

Lesson 6 — Planning Before Writing

Definition: An outline is a map of your writing.
Simple meaning: Introduction → 3 points → Conclusion. Never start without a map.
Practice: Make an outline for "Benefits of reading".

Lesson 7 — Writing a Strong Introduction

Definition: Introduction tells the reader what, why and how.
Formula: Hook + Background + Question + Plan.
Practice: Write 1 introduction in 5 sentences using that formula.

Lesson 8 — Writing Strong Paragraphs

Definition: One paragraph = One main idea.
Formula: Topic sentence → Explain → Example/Evidence → Link to next idea.
Practice: Write 1 perfect paragraph using this formula.

Lesson 9 — Building an Argument

Definition: An argument is a claim + reason + evidence.
Example: Claim: Students need sleep. Reason: Brain needs rest. Evidence: Studies show tired students forget faster.
Practice: Build 3 arguments on one topic.

Lesson 10 — Using Evidence Correctly

Definition: Evidence is facts, data, examples or expert views that support you.
Rule: Never say "everyone knows". Show proof.
Practice: Add evidence to 3 claims you wrote before.

Lesson 11 — Structure: Main Body

Definition: Main body is where you prove your points in order.
Order: Strongest point first, then second, then third. Each point gets its own section.
Practice: Arrange your 3 points in best order.
